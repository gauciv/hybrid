# Published testimony from Filipino domestic workers

We could not interview OFWs directly, so the problem statement is grounded in **published, verbatim
testimony**. Names are pseudonyms given by the source. Quotes are copied exactly; `[…]` edits are the source's.

**Primary source:** Amnesty International, *"Once we step in their homes, we are no longer human":
Testimonies of Filipino women domestic workers in Saudi Arabia*, index **MDE 23/1197/2026**, July 2026.
19 Filipino women who returned from Saudi Arabia, mostly between 2023 and 2026.
Report page: https://www.amnesty.org/en/documents/MDE23/1197/2026/en/
News release: https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/07/global-filipino-domestic-workers-exploited-in-saudi-arabia/

> In the video, say *"testimonies published by Amnesty International"*, never *"we interviewed"*.

## The contract is ignored → why Kontrata exists

> "If you are a domestic helper there [in Saudi Arabia], the boss is the contract - whatever they want, they will get"
> **Elena**, former domestic worker. Report p.17, *Conclusion: "Whatever they want"*

> "All that is written there [in the contract], is not followed. For example, it says 'the work will be finished
> after eight hours', but it is not. Then, 'you will rest three hours'. But no, there is no rest…"
> **Gloria**. Report p.11. Amnesty: *"what was written in their contracts was secondary to what their employers
> required at any given moment"*, *"a broader pattern described by most of the women interviewed."*

## Passport confiscation → not knowing it's illegal

> "my employer just said "I am going to take your passport"."
> "It seemed to be just the way it is done there. I didn't reject because it was my first time being abroad"
> **Eliza**, arrived June 2023. Report p.12. Amnesty: *"Nearly all the women interviewed said they were told to
> hand over their passports to their employers soon after arrival."*

**This is the core insight for the product:** Eliza didn't refuse because she *didn't know* it was not allowed.
DMW Advisory 25-2025 states passports "must remain with the worker at all times" (`docs/sources/rules/DMW-ADVISORY-25-2025.txt`).
Knowing the rule *before* departure is exactly what Kontrata gives.

## No rest day

> "No day off, I worked for two years straight without a day off."
> **Adelina**. Report p.11. Amnesty: *"Nearly all of those interviewed said their employer would also regularly
> deny them a weekly rest day, often for the duration of their contract."*

> Employer to Gemma, when she asked for one day off a month: "Why do you need a day off? … my house is not that big".
> **Gemma** (recounting), report p.11.

## Work outside the contract

> "including my employer's, their mother's, siblings' and other relatives. I would do the work in all five houses."
> **Hana**. Amnesty: *"Other women said their employer made them work in multiple households, contrary to the terms
> of their employment agreements."*

## Scale (Amnesty's findings)

- "Working hours stretched well beyond legal limits, from 14 up to 21 hours each day."
- "The lack of freedom of movement, exacerbated by widespread passport confiscation, legal restrictions on them
  leaving the country without permission…"
